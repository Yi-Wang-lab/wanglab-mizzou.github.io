Advanced manufacturing for medicine and smart industry

The Wang Lab in the Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering at the University of Missouri develops advanced micro/nanomanufacturing processes and uses them to build biomedical devices and intelligent manufacturing systems.

Our work combines ultrasonic vibration, electric fields, and lasers with 3D printing to fabricate high-precision functional structures, from flexible circuits and laser-induced graphene sensors to neural probes for brain–machine interfaces. We also develop human-centered smart manufacturing systems that connect machines, data, and people through mixed reality, the industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), AI, and digital twins.

We collaborate with clinicians, neuroscientists, and industry partners to move these technologies toward real use. Our research is supported by the National Science Foundation, including an NSF CAREER Award, and by state, clinical, industry, and university sponsors.

News

2026

  • Sep We started a new industry-sponsored project with Sentio Biosciences to develop an automated loss-in-weight twin-screw powder feeding system.
  • Apr Dr. Wang received the NSF CAREER Award. The five-year project studies ultrasonic vibration-enhanced anti-gravity electrohydrodynamic (EHD) printing for high-precision functional structures.
  • Jan A new project funded by the American Association for Hand Surgery will develop a high-resolution electrode array to detect sensory nerve injury during surgery, together with our clinical collaborators.
  • New papers in Materials Horizons, on in-situ additive manufacturing of laser-induced graphene embedded in PEEK, and in Virtual and Physical Prototyping, on ultrasonic-assisted screw-extrusion 3D printing.
  • Dr. Wang co-organized the Bio-Manufacturing Symposium at ASME MSEC 2026.

2025

  • Apr We filed a U.S. non-provisional patent application for an ultrasonic vibration-assisted printhead (No. 19/195,123).
  • Dr. Wang was named the Spring 2025 Outstanding Professor in Industrial and Systems Engineering.
  • Three best paper awards: the FAIM 2025 Best Paper Award, the ACTG 2025 Best Student Paper Award, and the DHM 2025 Best Paper Award at HCI International for a collaborative study of augmented reality–based biomechanics education.
  • Our dual-gate organic electrochemical transistors on laser-scribed graphene, which detect dopamine and glutamate, were published in Advanced Materials Technologies.

2024

  • Sep Support from the MU Coulter Biomedical Accelerator to develop an implantable electronic nerve stimulator that accelerates and monitors nerve healing, with clinical collaborators at MU.
  • Jul A new NSF project with NC State University and the University of North Carolina will develop flexible, fMRI-compatible neural probes with organic semiconductor-based multimodal sensors for closed-loop neuromodulation.
  • Dr. Wang received the Bloss Faculty Enhancement Grant Award from the University of Missouri.
  • Dr. Wang co-chaired the Smart Manufacturing and Cyber-Physical Systems track at SME NAMRC 52.
  • Two papers in Manufacturing Letters: ultrasonic vibration-assisted high-resolution EHD printing, and a human-centered platform for holographic control of 3D printers.

2023

  • Jul Mizzou began building an Industry 4.0 laboratory and certification program with support from the MOExcels Workforce Initiative of the Missouri Department of Higher Education and Workforce Development. Dr. Wang is a Co-PI.
  • Mar A College of Engineering Research Seed Grant will support 3D customizable, multifunctional neural microelectrode arrays made by EHD 3D printing.

2022

  • Dec A University of Missouri Research Council Grant will support a customizable, multifunctional neural probe for individualized neuroscience studies and precision medicine.
  • Mar Dr. Wang joined the Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering at the University of Missouri as an Assistant Professor, and the Wang Lab was founded.
